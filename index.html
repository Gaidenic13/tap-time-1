MVP honesty

A passive NFC tap proves that a phone reached the tag. It does not, by itself, prove who held the phone. Production should add authenticated employee accounts, rotating signed links, audit logs, and a dedicated reader where stronger assurance is required.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from a badge reader?

A badge reader needs powered hardware, issued credentials, and IT integration. TapTime is a passive tag in a printed case: employees use the phone already in their pocket, and the web flow does the identifying.

Can someone tap in for a colleague?

A passive tap proves a phone reached the tag—not who held it. The pilot accepts that honestly; production adds authenticated accounts, rotating signed links, and audit logs to close the gap.

Do employees need to install an app?

No. Tapping opens a normal web page. Any NFC-capable phone from roughly the last eight years works out of the box.

What happens if the checkpoint is damaged or lost?

Print another case and write a fresh tag—about €5 and one coffee break. Old links can be revoked server-side at any time.

What data does TapTime store?

Only the event: who, where, when, in or out. No location tracking, no photos, no biometrics—the device can't sense anything at all.

How do we cover multiple entrances or sites?

Print one case per doorway and write each tag with its own signed link. Every checkpoint identifies its location, so records stay clean across buildings and sites.

Can we put our own branding on the case?

Yes—the wordmark is part of the printable model, not a sticker. Swap the recessed text in the CAD script and every case you print carries your brand.

Can records feed into payroll?

The pilot produces clean per-person, per-day records you can review or export. The production path in the proposal adds authenticated accounts and integration points for payroll systems.
